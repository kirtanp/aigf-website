**Florian Tramèr** is an assistant professor of Computer Science at [ETH Zürich](https://ethz.ch/en.html). He leads the [SPY Lab](https://www.spylab.ai/), and is a member of the [Information Security Institute](https://informationsecurity.ethz.ch/) and of [ZISC](https://zisc.ethz.ch/), and an associated faculty of the [ETHZ AI Center](https://ai.ethz.ch/).

His research interests lie in Computer Security, Machine Learning and Cryptography. In his current work, he studies the worst-case behavior of Deep Learning systems from an adversarial perspective, to understand and mitigate long-term threats to the safety and privacy of users.

His work has been featured in [The Economist](https://www.economist.com/science-and-technology/2023/04/05/it-doesnt-take-much-to-make-machine-learning-algorithms-go-awry), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-021-00530-0), [Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/alarmed-tech-leaders-call-ai-research-pause), [Communications of the ACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/208289-how-to-steal-the-mind-of-an-ai/fulltext), [Wired](https://www.wired.com/2016/09/how-to-steal-an-ai/) and [the Swiss news (in french)](https://www.rts.ch/info/sciences-tech/technologies/13973323-intelligence-artificielle-ange-ou-demon.html).

He received his PhD from [Stanford University](https://www.stanford.edu/) under the supervision of [Dan Boneh](https://crypto.stanford.edu/~dabo/). After graduating, he spent one year at [Google Brain](https://research.google/teams/brain/).
