[**Thibault Schrepel**](http://thibaultschrepel.com/en/), LL.M., research centers around the dynamics and regulation of digital markets, which he explores through the lens of complexity science.

He is an Associate Professor of Law at the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam University ([Amsterdam Law & Technology Institute](http://alti.amsterdam/)), and a [Faculty Affiliate](https://law.stanford.edu/directory/thibault-schrepel/) at Stanford University ([CodeX Center](https://law.stanford.edu/codex-the-stanford-center-for-legal-informatics/)), where he founded the “[Computational Antitrust](http://www.computationalantitrust.com/)” project that brings together over 65 antitrust agencies. Thibault is also the founder of the [Network Law Review](http://www.networklawreview.org/), and the host of the “[Scaling Theory](https://open.spotify.com/show/7o5GqRfTOzyCRABkvs6RpM)” podcast.

Thibault holds research and teaching positions at the University Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne and Sciences Po Paris. He is an alumnus of the Berkman Center at Harvard University, a member of the scientific board of the French Superior Audiovisual Council, and a blockchain expert appointed to the World Economic Forum and the World Bank.

In past years, Thibault received the “Academic Excellence” Global Competition Review Award, which recognizes “an academic competition specialist who has made an outstanding contribution to competition policy.” He has [published](https://thibaultschrepel.com/en/publications/) a first manuscript (Bruylant) on the subject of “predatory innovation in antitrust law” and articles at Harvard University, Stanford, MIT, Oxford, NYU, Berkeley, and Georgetown, among others. His second book, “[Blockchain + Antitrust](https://www.e-elgar.com/shop/gbp/blockchain-antitrust-9781800885523.html)”, was published in September 2021 (Edward Elgar).

In recent years, Thibault has focused most of his research on [blockchain antitrust](https://blockchainantitrust.com/), [computational antitrust](http://www.computationalantitrust.com/), and complexity theory. He has written the world’s most downloaded antitrust articles on SSRN in 2018 (“[The Blockchain Antitrust Paradox](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3193576)”), 2019 (“[Collusion by Blockchain and Smart Contracts](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3315182)”), 2020 (“[Blockchain Code as Antitrust](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3597399)”), 2021 (“[Computational Antitrust: An Introduction and Research Agenda](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3766960)”), and 2022 (“[Complexity-Minded Antitrust](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4050536)”). In 2023 and 2024, three of his articles ranked among the top 10 most downloaded antitrust papers on SSRN globally, with one achieving the highest number of downloads in 2024 (“[Measuring the Openness of AI Foundation Models: Competition and Policy Implications](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4827358)”). He has recently co-edited “[Artificial Intelligence and Competition Policy](https://www.concurrences.com/en/all-books/artificial-intelligence-and-competition-policy)” (Concurrences, 2024).
